## 1. Global Competitor & Pricing Research (Scott's Request)

Scott asked for a thorough search of companies around the world selling similar products, with their pricing, to spot any gaps in the market. That research is complete.

**What was checked:** Over 20 real, currently operating companies selling oil lamp chimneys, shades, pressure lamp glass, oil lamp books, and reproduction enamel advertising signs — across the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, Germany, and South Africa. The last three countries were added specifically because they showed up as real visitors in the site's own traffic data, not just guessed at. Every price quoted came directly from each company's own website, with the source link included so it can be double-checked.

**Main finding — pricing is right where it should be.** Comparing the site's current chimney, shade, and glass prices against these 20+ companies, nothing is priced too high or too low. It's sitting right in the middle of the market.

**A second finding worth flagging — the advertising signs need a decision from Scott.** The enamel advertising signs are listed as "Original" at $890 each. Checked against the market, that price is too high to be a fair price for a reproduction (real reproductions of this style typically sell for $260–490), but too low to be a believable price for a genuine 100+ year old original sign (those typically sell for $1,800–5,000+ for known brands). Before finalizing anything on these signs, it would help to confirm with Scott whether they're genuine antiques or reproductions, so the listing and price can match exactly.

**A real opportunity found:** none of the 20+ companies checked sell lamp parts, complete lamps, and advertising signs all together the way this store does — most only do one of the three. That's a genuine advantage worth playing up more in the site's marketing.

**Delivered to Scott:** a client-ready report (plus a PDF copy for easy sharing) covering all companies and pricing found, and a ready-to-use spreadsheet with suggested prices for all 22 lamp parts currently showing $0. A short holding reply was sent to Scott letting him know the full report would follow later today, so it doesn't look rushed.

## 2. How the Site's Tracking Tools Are Set Up

A question came up about how Google Analytics, Google's shopping-listing tool (Merchant Center), and Bing's search tool are currently registered — all three are under a personal account for now, not Scott's.

**Clarified:** none of these three tools hold or move any money — they're just measurement and listing tools. The actual sales money goes straight from Shopify into Scott's own bank account, completely separate from these tools, and that hasn't changed. For now, it's fine to leave these three tools registered as they are; the plan is to eventually hand full ownership of them over to Scott (a simple task whenever it's time), so he's never locked out of his own site's data down the road.

## 3. Notification Bell — Investigated and Upgrade Planned

Followed up on why the admin notification bell only alerts about new customer sign-ups, but not new customer messages (like the recent inquiry from Allan Jeffery, which has since been replied to). 

**What was found:** the notification bell was only ever connected to the store's sales system (new orders, low stock, new customers) — it was never connected to the messaging side of the dashboard at all. Not a bug, just something that was never built yet.

**What's planned:** a design was worked out to add three new types of alerts to the bell — new customer messages, new product reviews waiting to be approved, and restock requests (customers asking to be told when a sold-out item is back). The design has been reviewed and approved. Building it is scheduled for the next work session.
